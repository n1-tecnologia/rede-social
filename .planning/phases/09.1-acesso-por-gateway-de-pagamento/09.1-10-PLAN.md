<planning_decisions>
1. **The e-mail is the key of an Acessos row.** The list's `id` is the normalised e-mail and every action takes `{ email }` in the body (never a path segment, so an address with `+` or `@` has no encoding trap and the log line of a route never contains it).
2. **State comes from SQL.** The list calls `app.access_effective(tenant, email)` for each row of the page; there is no second implementation of D-489 in TypeScript, so the screen, the gate and the hooks cannot disagree.
3. **Two independent manual rows.** `block` and `release` never edit each other's row; `unblock` and `remove-release` delete one specific row. That is what makes "Desbloquear" a restore (plan 02, planning decision 2).
4. **`grant` is three short steps, not one long transaction.** Read and validate (admin lane), call the store's definer in the admin's own tenant lane (it needs the `admin_tenant` claim by design), then a guarded admin update of the event row; a crash between steps 2 and 3 is repaired by the idempotent re-click (the definer answers `already_active`).
5. **One link source.** `accessCheckoutUrl(tx, tenantId)` in `tenancy/access.ts` is the single reader of "the oldest access product with a link, only while the module is on and the gate is on"; the subscription endpoint, the public tenant and the join state all call it.
6. **The web line is exhaustiveness only.** Adding `access_required` to the join states makes the `/participar` switch non-exhaustive; the one added `case` keeps the build green and changes no visible behaviour until plan 15.
</planning_decisions>
