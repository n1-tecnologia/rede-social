<objective>
The brain of the integration: the job that turns a recorded delivery into a change of what a person may do. After this plan a signed delivery actually grants a person access (even before they have an account), takes it away on a late, canceled, refunded or charged-back subscription, gives it back on a renewal, unlocks and revokes a store product at the amount paid, and tells the rest of the system exactly once when a member was cut. Every ambiguous case lands in a visible state instead of a guess.

Task 1 is the tracer: ONE subscription's whole life (approved, canceled, renewed) through the real route, the job, the grants and a real gated request, with the pure order-of-events policy and the emission. Task 2 adds the store branch, the visible states and the tenant pinning. Task 3 proves idempotency, ordering and concurrency.

<planning_decisions>
1. **One transaction for the state, one flush after it.** The grant, the order, the event row's new state and the before/after access reads commit together; only then are domain events emitted, so a subscriber can never see a cut that a rollback erased.
2. **The policy is pure.** `decideAccessWrite` and `decideStoreWrite` take the effect, whether the sale is recurring, the stored row and the timestamps and return the write to make and the outcome; the SQL in `apply.ts` only executes it. The table of cases is the executable specification of the order-of-events rule and the thing to edit if the real capture (plan 20) changes the semantics.
3. **Access rows are keyed by the sale, not the person.** The key is `(tenant, provider, external_ref)`, so a person with two products has two rows and the effective state is computed in SQL (plan 02); an e-mail-only key would let a cancel of one product cut another.
4. **The cut transition is computed, not assumed.** Reading `app.access_state` before and after the write is what makes the emission exact: a renewal while already active, a cancel of a second product while another is active, and a gate-off tenant all emit nothing.
5. **System context stays in the kernel.** The payments job has its own zero-user system lane constant; it does not import the notifications module's.
6. **No retries of business outcomes.** `no_membership`, `unknown_product`, `ignored` and `stale` are final states, not failures; only an exception marks `failed`.
</planning_decisions>
