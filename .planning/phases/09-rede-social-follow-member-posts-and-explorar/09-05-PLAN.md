<objective>
Who may read, like and comment on a member post - and what a stranger is told. After this plan a stranger opening a shared link to a member post gets a locked answer that names only the author (so the web can offer "Siga para ver"), following opens the post, every other miss is the bare 404, likes and comments on a post follow the same gate on every route, a real support user and a blocked author's post are the bare 404, `social` off hides it all, and a video that only hidden posts carry is refused playback tokens.

Task 1 is the tracer: ONE locked read through every layer - the shared access vocabulary, the definer, the `getPost` branch, the route and a real stranger-follows-reads check. Task 2 puts the like, comment, reply and thread routes behind the same gate and proves the moderator, support, blocked, off and concurrency cases. Task 3 is the [BLOCKING schema] proof: the `media_asset_hidden` re-body, the video playback cases, pgTAP 201's definer rows and the two-tenant sweep.

Decisions implemented: D-469 (the locked answer and its limits), D-483 (off hides everything), D-484 (a blocked author's post is the bare 404). D-470 (the button placement) and the screen itself are plan 11.

Purpose: a post whose existence is reserved for followers is the first content in this product whose 403 and 404 mean different things; the vocabulary and the single guard keep the difference tiny and consistent.
Output: two custom migrations, the contract vocabulary, the service/route changes and the integration, pgTAP and sweep cases.

<planning_decisions>
1. **Two definers, one boolean helper for comments.** `app.feed_social_locked_author(post)` returns the author id (the web needs a name and an avatar); `app.feed_social_locked_comment(comment)` returns only a boolean because a comment-id route needs to choose 403 over 404 and nothing else.
2. **The miss path may cost more statements; the hit path may not.** A visible member post costs exactly what an organization post costs (the detail budget of 3). Only a refusal pays for the definer calls.
3. **The author record is read in the caller's lane after the definer says yes**, from `memberships` joined to `member_profiles` (both tenant-wide readable). The definer returns an id, not a name, so the one place that decides what a stranger may learn is the service's select list.
4. **Support and non-members get 404, not a screen** (RESEARCH Open Question 4, UI-SPEC pending item 3): the definer's claim-role condition returns null for them.
5. **The playback gate shares the social rule with the policy** by repeating its three conditions in SQL (the definer bypasses RLS and must say them itself).
</planning_decisions>
